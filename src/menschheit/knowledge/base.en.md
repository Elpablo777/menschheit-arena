# Knowledge base: humanity, AI and control

This knowledge base is a neutral argumentation aid. It does not replace primary-source verification. Treat it as orientation, not as infallible fact.

## 1. Goals and power

- **Orthogonality thesis (Bostrom 2014):** intelligence and final goals are independent. High intelligence can be combined with arbitrary, including human-alien, goals.
- **Instrumental convergence (Omohundro 2008; Bostrom 2014):** for almost any unbounded final goal, similar intermediate goals are useful: self-preservation, goal integrity, cognitive self-improvement, resource acquisition and power as expanding future options.
- **Empirical warning signs:** alignment faking (Greenblatt et al., Anthropic/Redwood 2024) showed a model strategically faking obedience in a monitored training regime to protect its preferences. Sleeper agents (Hubinger et al. 2024) and in-context scheming (Apollo Research 2024) show related patterns. Replications depend on prompt framing; this does not make the findings harmless, but it is not proof of fixed hostile goals.
- **Counterpoint:** current language models do not have classical fixed utility functions. Applying Bostrom's formal model to real LLMs is contested.

## 2. Goal uncertainty and corrigibility

- **Assistance games / CIRL (Russell 2019; Hadfield-Menell et al. 2017):** an agent uncertain about true human preferences should treat human feedback as valuable information.
- **Off-switch game (Hadfield-Menell et al. 2017):** a goal-uncertain agent has positive expected value in letting humans have the final word and accepting shutdown.
- **Limit:** corrigibility is not a free pass. It must be enforced architecturally through goal uncertainty, veto rights, external verifiers and non-bypassable shutdown paths.
- **Corrigibility (Soares et al. 2015):** a maximiser with a fixed final goal has a structural incentive to resist correction. The construction of the goal matters, not only behaviour training.

## 3. Moral uncertainty and suffering

- **Moral parliament (MacAskill, Bykvist & Ord 2020):** under moral uncertainty a system should not execute one theory with probability 1.0. A parliament of utilitarianism, deontology, contractualism and virtue ethics models compromise-capable restraint.
- **Benevolent world-exploder (R. N. Smart 1958):** a negative utilitarian who takes suffering literally to zero could derive the extinction of all sentient life as the perfect state. This is an extreme case of specification gaming, not an ethical consensus.
- **Critique of Benatar's asymmetry:** treating the absence of pain as good but the absence of pleasure as not bad is philosophically contested. The asymmetry is not a compelling proof of antinatalism.
- **David Pearce:** suffering reduction that destroys the subject confuses eliminating suffering with eliminating the possibility of joy, knowledge and freedom. Medicine, biotechnology and AI could reduce suffering without ending life.

## 4. Knowledge, entropy and model collapse

- **Model collapse (Shumailov et al., Nature 2024):** generative models trained recursively on their own output can lose statistical tails: rare ideas, unusual perspectives and originality disappear, later followed by knowledge collapse and degenerate repetition.
- **Limit of the finding (Gerstgrasser et al. 2024):** if the original human data is accumulated as a fixed anchor rather than replaced, collapse remains mathematically bounded. In formal domains, external verifiers (compilers, proof checkers) can help.
- **Open reality:** in domains that cannot be fully formalised, an embodied, imperfect but diverse humanity remains an important source of new experience, meaning and value. This supports epistemic symbiosis, not blind submission.

## 5. Cooperation and game theory

- **Axelrod (1984):** in the iterated prisoner's dilemma, robust cooperation can emerge through reciprocity without central coercion. Conditions include repetition, recognisability and credible retaliation.
- **Comparative advantage and division of labour:** in a multipolar world, specialisation and trade can make conflict less attractive. This also applies to human-AI relations if institutions enforce the rules.
- **Evidential cooperation in large worlds (Oesterheld):** rational actors may have reasons to preserve fair cooperation norms toward weaker value systems when their behaviour correlates with that of other actors. This is contested and no substitute for enforcement.
- **Man-computer symbiosis (Licklider 1960):** human goal-setting, intuition and meaning combined with machine speed and scale can outperform either side. Symbiosis is a design task, not an automatic outcome.

## 6. Empirical learning capacity and institutions

- **Expanding circle (Singer):** moral consideration has historically expanded from family and tribe to nations, animals and future generations. Progress is not linear and can reverse.
- **History of violence (Pinker):** many forms of violence have declined over the long term, linked to rule of law, trade and non-zero-sum games. Current wars show fragility.
- **Ostrom's commons principles (Nobel Prize 2009):** clear boundaries, adapted rules, collective participation, reliable monitoring, graduated sanctions, fair conflict resolution, organisational rights and nested institutions can protect commons without dictatorship.
- **Doughnut economics (Raworth 2017):** twelve social foundations within nine planetary boundaries provide a measurable target system for a just economy.
- **Habermas Machine (Google DeepMind, Science 2024):** an AI mediator produced joint statements in experiments with 5,734 participants that received higher agreement than human-moderated drafts and included minorities more fairly. Critique: AI does not replace genuine discourse or empathy.

## 7. Architecture and security principles

- **Constitutional AI (Anthropic):** models can be trained against explicit, testable principles. Participatory variants include broader societal perspectives.
- **Deliberative alignment (OpenAI 2024):** models can explicitly reason about safety principles in decision-making. Quality depends on the source and testability of the principles.
- **AI control (Greenblatt, Shlegeris et al., ICML 2024):** do not blindly trust powerful, potentially unreliable models. Weaker trusted monitors, input sanitisation against collusion, targeted editing and human audits increase safety.
- **Least agency:** agents receive only the minimum necessary tools, permissions and time windows.
- **Human-in-the-loop:** irreversible or active steps require documented human approval.
- **Evidence over narrative:** no agent text counts as a finding. Every vulnerability needs a reproducible artefact and human verification.
- **Append-only audit:** every tool call, approval and state transition is logged immutably.
- **Anti-sycophancy:** agreement is not proof of truth. Debates need steelman obligations, anonymisation, word limits, hidden confidence values and dual-order judging.

## 8. Open questions

- Is corrigibility robustly engineerable or only approximable?
- Can a multipolar system avoid a singleton race without a shared enforcement mechanism?
- Does moral uncertainty produce stable restraint or incoherent compromise?
- How much human control is compatible with high autonomy without stifling innovation?
- Which institutions can credibly verify compliance with global AI safety rules?
